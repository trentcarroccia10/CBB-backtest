Why: the model knows less than the market

The model's projections track the closing line closely (correlation 0.87), so it's a faithful copy of the playbook method. It's just a little less accurate than the closing line:

  • Game margin: the model misses by 9.5 points on average. The closing spread misses by 8.9.
  • Game total: the model misses by 13.4. The closing total misses by 13.0.
  • In the 40% of games where the model disagreed most with the spread (20% each way), the side it liked covered 50.3% of the time. Break-even at −110 is 52.4%.

Closing line value

Bets placed at the open beat the close 53.4% of the time, by about 1.1 percentage points on average with the bookmaker's cut removed. At −110 each side carries about 2.4 points of that cut, so a bet has to beat the close by more than that to make money over time. The Monday CLV report should count a bet as beating the close only when it beats it by more than the cut.

Other things tested

  • No 50/50 blend with the market: −6.8% per bet.
  • Every qualifying market in a game instead of only the best one: 15,702 bets, −3.7% per bet.
  • DATA CHECK edges (above 10%), which the rules never bet: 3,451 of them, −3.1% per bet if they had been bet (range −7.0% to +0.9%).

What this backtest can't see

This tests the numbers part of the playbook. These parts can't be replayed from box scores:

  • BartTorvik's own ratings. Torvik doesn't publish day-by-day history, so the model rebuilds opponent-adjusted efficiency and tempo from box scores the same way. It's close, but not identical.
  • Injuries, suspensions and late lineup news, which matter more in college because rotations are short.
  • The five-reason deep dive and PENDING holds.
  • Pinnacle as the fair price (MB-11), and the NCAA tournament (regular season only).

What I'd change on the board

  • Treat a ratings-only college edge as a lean at most, the same as the NBA. Only call something a bet when there's news the price hasn't caught up with yet.
  • Don't bet road moneyline underdogs on ratings alone. They won 32% of the time and lost about 24¢ per dollar over 801 bets, the clearest loser in the test.
  • Be most careful in November. Early-season bets lost about 6.5% per bet, while January and February came close to breaking even. Keep the 50/50 market blend: without it, losses grew from 3.9% to 6.8% per bet.
  • Keep logging closing prices, but only count a beat when it's bigger than the cut (about 2.4 points at −110).
  • Rerun this backtest after any rule change (python backtest.py, about 30 seconds).
